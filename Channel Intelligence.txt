PATTERN 1: "What Happened?" is the channel's strongest confirmed format —
with one valid head-to-head data point so far.

  Valid comparison (both launched in this window):
  — "From Arkansas to Alabama State! What Happened?" → 10,007 views, 4.76% CTR
  — "He Tore His ACL and Got Cut by the NFL. Then This Happened." → 56 views, 1.65% CTR

  The Arkansas/Tavaris video outperformed Ian Wheeler's video by ~180x in views
  and nearly 3x in CTR. Both are new-release videos so this IS a fair comparison.

  NOTE: The Oregon, LSU, and Savannah State "What Happened?" videos showing
  31/30/26 views in this window are OLD videos (Aug-Sep 2025) that had their
  launch spike long before this data window. We cannot use their tail traffic
  to assess how the "What Happened?" format performs at launch for those subjects.
  That comparison is invalid until we have their first-28-days data.

  What we CAN say from the one valid comparison: The "What Happened?" framing
  with a recognizable school name in the title (Arkansas) vastly outperformed
  a generic NFL emotional hook (Ian Wheeler) in both CTR and YouTube
  distribution. Source school name recognition is a hypothesis worth testing,
  but needs more launch-window data to confirm.

  Scout instruction: Continue tracking "What Happened?" videos at launch.
  Erick Hunter and Andrew Body will add two more valid data points next month.

PATTERN 2: EA College Football content is the reliable #2 lane — and it
builds a MORE LOYAL audience than the story content.

  Data: CF27 video (4,605 views, 5.59% CTR on 57k impressions) is the only
  other video generating meaningful traffic. More importantly, 26% of CF27
  viewers are returning visitors vs. only 9% for the Arkansas "What Happened?"
  video. The older Teambuilder videos (Pt.1 and Pt.2) show 45-55% returning
  viewers — the gaming audience keeps coming back.

  Interpretation: HBCU story content acquires new viewers. Gaming content
  retains them and builds a subscriber base. Both lanes are necessary.

  Script Writer instruction: At least one EA/gaming video per major game
  release cycle is non-negotiable for channel health. These are lower-effort
  videos that punch above their weight on subscriber retention.

PATTERN 3: High CTR alone means nothing without impressions to back it up.

  Data:
  — Teambuilder Pt.2: 10.98% CTR × 410 impressions = 70 views
  — CF27: 5.59% CTR × 57,469 impressions = 4,605 views
  — Arkansas/Tavaris: 4.76% CTR × 116,494 impressions = 10,007 views

  The CTR on the Teambuilder videos (9-11%) is genuinely excellent. YouTube
  just isn't showing those thumbnails to many people — likely because the
  videos are older and from a smaller-audience period of the channel.

  Interpretation: Focus thumbnails and titles on maintaining CTR above 4.5%
  at scale. A 4.76% CTR on 116k impressions is more valuable than a 10%
  CTR on 400 impressions.

PATTERN 4: Shorts are getting quiet traction through search/hashtags, not
the Browse feed — a separate traffic source worth maintaining.

  Data: Deacon Jones (72 views), Willie Lanier (42 views), Rayfield Wright
  (13 views) all have 0 impressions. These views come entirely from
  search or channel browse — YouTube isn't pushing them via Browse/Home feed.

  Interpretation: The Shorts aren't competing with long-form content for
  algorithm distribution. They're building search presence for HBCU history
  figures. Keep publishing them at low frequency (2-3/month) as a brand-
  awareness play without sacrificing long-form output.

WHAT THE DATA CONTRADICTS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

HYPOTHESIS (NOT YET CONFIRMED): "Source school name recognition drives
'What Happened?' performance more than the format itself."

DATA: The only valid comparison in this window is Arkansas/Tavaris (10,007
views, 4.76% CTR) vs. Ian Wheeler's ACL/NFL framing (56 views, 1.65% CTR).
The Oregon, LSU, and Savannah State "What Happened?" videos can't be used —
they're old, and we're only seeing 28-day tail traffic, not launch performance.

This hypothesis is plausible and worth guiding content decisions, but needs
1-2 more "What Happened?" launch cycles (Erick Hunter, Andrew Body) to confirm.
If those videos also perform well with recognizable source school names, the
pattern is real. If they don't, we need to look elsewhere for the variable.

ASSUMPTION OVERTURNED: "The 'He Tore His ACL' story framing is strong because
it follows a proven emotional hook format."

REALITY: "He Tore His ACL and Got Cut by the NFL. Then This Happened." (Ian
Wheeler, Jul 2 '26) has a 1.65% CTR — the lowest of any video with significant
impressions (2,177). The generic NFL injury framing isn't resonating. The HBCU
angle should be front-loaded in the title, not buried after the emotional hook.
Compare to Arkansas/Tavaris Jackson where the HBCU school is in the title.

SCOUT PRIORITY ORDER (updated from data)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Priority 1 — "What Happened?" stories where the subject came from a TOP-25
or Power 4 brand-name program. The bigger the name of the source school,
the more YouTube search pull the video gets. SEC > ACC > Big Ten > Big 12
for name recognition.

Priority 2 — EA College Football / Gaming content tied to a new release
or major gaming news. CF27 (5.59% CTR, 4,605 views) proves this lane is
productive and builds a returning audience.

Priority 3 — Classic-era HBCU history stories with a famous name in the
title (Jerry Rice, Doug Williams, Deacon Jones). The famous name provides
search traffic from outside the HBCU community.

Priority 4 — Current advocacy/news stories (undrafted prospects, NIL, etc.)
These haven't cracked meaningful views yet but are important for channel
identity. Keep them short (under 8 minutes). Do NOT use generic NFL injury
framing — lead with the HBCU school and player name in the title.

Priority 5 — Shorts (15-60 seconds) on legendary HBCU figures. Low effort,
builds search presence, doesn't interfere with long-form algorithm.

DE-PRIORITIZED until title/thumbnail strategy improves:
— Stories where the source school is not a widely recognized brand name
— Standalone gaming news (EA site shutdowns, roster updates) without a
  specific HBCU angle — these underperform even with the gaming audience

SCRIPT WRITER NOTES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

TITLE FORMULA that is working at scale:
  "From [Big-Name School] to [HBCU]! What Happened?"

  This format generates 4.76% CTR at 116k impressions — strong enough for
  YouTube to keep distributing. Keep both school names in the title.

TITLE FORMAT to avoid:
  Generic emotional hooks without an HBCU school name ("He Tore His ACL and
  Got Cut by the NFL. Then This Happened." = 1.65% CTR). The HBCU name
  should appear in the title, not just in the thumbnail or description.

HOOK STYLE for "What Happened?" videos:
  The Arkansas/Tavaris Jackson video holds viewers to 3:03 average on a 10:50
  video (28% retention). Open with the question immediately — don't explain
  what the video will cover, raise the mystery. Viewers who click "What
  Happened?" already bought in; reward them by getting to the story fast.

OPTIMAL VIDEO LENGTH based on data:
  — "What Happened?" format: 8-12 minutes (650-750 seconds) performs best
  — Gaming content: 10-13 minutes is fine (CF27 at 756 sec, 5.59% CTR)
  — Avoid videos under 5 minutes in the long-form format — they don't have
    enough watch time to trigger meaningful algorithm distribution

END SCREENS: The Arkansas video drove 14 end-screen clicks (the most of any
video). Use end screens consistently and point to other "What Happened?"
videos — viewers who finished one story are most likely to click another.
