PATTERN 3: The "What Happened?" series works — but impressions drive
view counts more than source school name recognition.

  All four "What Happened?" videos performed above the channel average,
  but at very different scales:

    LSU to Southern:         18,850 views | 224,856 impressions | 4.39% CTR
    Arkansas to Alabama St.: 10,007 views | 116,494 impressions | 4.76% CTR
    Oregon to Delaware St.:   4,487 views |  43,240 impressions | 7.51% CTR
    Power 4 to Savannah St.:    941 views |  17,049 impressions | 4.24% CTR

  Oregon actually had the HIGHEST CTR (7.51%) of any "What Happened?"
  video — but YouTube only served it 43k impressions vs 224k for LSU.
  If Oregon had gotten the same impressions as LSU, it would have been
  the top-performing video in the series.

  The view gap between videos is largely an impressions gap, not a CTR
  gap. What drives YouTube to give more impressions is still unclear from
  this data — could be timing, publishing cadence, watch time signals from
  early viewers, or topic search volume. More data needed.

  EXCEPTION: "Power 4 to Savannah State" (941 views, 4.24% CTR) clearly
  underperformed. The most likely cause: the title doesn't name the source
  school. Every other "What Happened?" title names both schools specifically.
  "Power 4" is vague and doesn't give casual viewers a recognizable hook.
  Always name the specific school in the title.

PATTERN 4: Avg view duration is stronger in the "What Happened?" series
than raw numbers suggest — they're shorter videos with solid retention.

  Retention rates (avg view duration / video length):
    Power 4 to Savannah State:  40% retention (1:28 on 3:41 video)

    LSU to Southern:            36% retention (1:45 on 4:50 video)

PATTERN 5: Shorts are a zero-impression traffic source — quietly useful.

  Deacon Jones, Willie Lanier, and Rayfield Wright Shorts all have
  0 impressions, meaning YouTube is not distributing them via Browse or
  Home feed at all. Yet they still accumulate views through direct channel
  browse, search, and hashtags. They don't compete with long-form for
  algorithm slots. Keep publishing 2-3 per month as a low-effort brand play.

WHAT DIDN'T WORK
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. IAN WHEELER — "He Tore His ACL and Got Cut by the NFL. Then This Happened."
   Published July 2, 2026 — 56 views, 1.65% CTR on 2,177 impressions.
   YouTube served this to 2,177 people and barely 2% clicked. That's a
   title/thumbnail problem. The HBCU connection is buried — the video reads
   like an NFL injury story, not an HBCU story. YouTube served it to sports
   fans who didn't see the HBCU angle and moved on.
   Consider retitling: "From the NFL to Delaware State! What Happened?"
   puts it in the proven format with recognizable framing.

2. NON-HBCU GAMING CONTENT — The generic CF25 tutorials (Coaching,
   Recruiting, Sights & Sounds) get views but don't convert to subscribers
   as efficiently as HBCU-specific gaming content. CF25 Coaching got 2,912
   views but only 12 subs (0.4% sub rate). Compare to Teambuilder Pt.1:
   21,036 views, 187 subs (0.9% sub rate). The HBCU angle doubles the
   subscriber conversion rate on gaming content.

3. THE SOLID CIRCLE PODCAST — 11 episodes, 315 total views, 0 subscribers
   net gained. The episodes that exist have good watch time (some over 15
   minutes avg) but essentially no distribution — most got <50 views. This
   format has not found an audience on YouTube. Not worth continued investment
   unless the strategy changes significantly.

4. VAGUE TITLES — Any video without a specific name or school in the title
   underperforms. "From the Power 4 to Savannah State" is the clearest
   example (941 views vs 4,487-18,850 for named-school versions). Be
   specific: name the player, name the school.

Priority 4 — Current HBCU news/advocacy.
  Low view counts currently but important for channel identity and search
  positioning for timely topics. Keep these under 8 minutes.

Priority 5 — Shorts on legendary HBCU figures.
  Zero-impression organic traffic. Low effort. Worth 2-3/month.

  — Titles that use "Power 4" without naming the school

SCRIPT WRITER NOTES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

TITLE FORMULAS THAT WORK AT SCALE:

  Gaming: "HBCUs in College Football [Year]? | [Specific Question]"
    → 11.28% CTR (HBCUs In CF26), 9.15% CTR (Team Builder SAVED CF26)
    The year in the title makes it feel current. The question format
    keeps it searchable.

  "What Happened?": "From [Specific School] to [Specific HBCU]! What Happened?"
    → Proven across 3 of 4 videos. Both school names required.
    The "!" before "What Happened?" signals a story with a twist.

TITLE FORMAT THAT IS FAILING:
  Generic emotional/NFL hook without HBCU framing = 1.65% CTR (Ian Wheeler).
  Whatever the story quality, the title has to signal the HBCU angle
  before the viewer clicks — not after.

VIDEO LENGTH:
  — "What Happened?": 5-11 minutes is fine (retention holds 28-40% across
    all lengths in this format)
  — Gaming: 10-15 minutes works well (CF25 Gameplay Deep Dive at 6:22 avg
    duration shows viewers will stay for long gaming content)
  — Aim for 8+ minutes on any long-form video to maximize ad revenue
    potential as the channel grows

HOOKS:
  The "What Happened?" format has proven retention (36-40%). The hook that's
  working: open on the mystery immediately. Don't set up context — raise the
  question. Viewers clicked because they want the answer; get there fast.

THUMBNAILS:
  The gaming thumbnails are outperforming story thumbnails in CTR (8-11%
  vs 4-5%). The gaming thumbnails likely feature bold text, game UI
  elements, and clear HBCU branding. Apply those visual principles to
  story thumbnails.

END SCREENS:
  The Arkansas/Tavaris video drove 14 end-screen clicks — most of any video.
  Consistently link to other "What Happened?" videos at the end. A viewer
  who finished one story is the most likely person to watch another.
