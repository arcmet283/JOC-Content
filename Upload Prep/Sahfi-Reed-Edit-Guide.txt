== SECTION A: PROJECT SETUP ==

STEP 1 — Create a new project:
Open Premiere Pro. At the top menu bar, click File > New > Project.
A window will appear asking you to name the project. Type: Sahfi Reed.
Choose where to save it (your Videos or Desktop folder is fine).
Click OK.

STEP 2 — Create a sequence:
At the top menu bar, click File > New > Sequence.
Click the Settings tab at the top of that window.
- Set Editing Mode to: Custom
- Set Timebase to: 30.00 frames/second
- Set Frame Size to: 1920 horizontal, 1080 vertical
- Set Pixel Aspect Ratio to: Square Pixels (1.0)
In the Sequence Name field, type: Main Edit.
Click OK.

STEP 3 — Import your footage:
File > Import (or Ctrl+I). Select all your video, photo, and music files at once (click one, hold Shift, click the last one). Click Open.

STEP 4 — Organize your Project panel:
Right-click in the Project panel (bottom-left). Choose New Bin. Create four bins: Talking Head, B-Roll, Music, Photos.
Drag each file into its correct bin.


== SECTION B: CUTTING YOUR TALKING HEAD FOOTAGE ==

STEP 1 — Load the clip into the Source Monitor:
Open the Talking Head bin. Double-click your clip. It opens in the Source Monitor (top-left panel).

STEP 2 — Find your good takes:
Press spacebar to play. Press I to mark the In point where a section starts, press O to mark the Out point where it ends.

STEP 3 — Send the clip to the Timeline:
Press the comma key (,) to insert the marked section into the Timeline (Video Track 1 / V1).

STEP 4 — Razor cuts:
Press C for the Razor tool, click to cut. Press V to switch back to Selection.

STEP 5 — Delete and close gaps:
Razor-cut around a bad section, select it, press Delete. Right-click the gap, choose Ripple Delete to close it.

WHAT IS B-ROLL:

== SECTION D: ADDING MUSIC ==

RECOMMENDED MUSIC MOOD FOR THIS VIDEO:
Unlike a systemic-injustice story, this is a determined-underdog-to-triumph arc — the music should feel motivational and building, not heavy or somber.

Phase 1 (0:00 to about 1:30 — the setup and the walk-on decision):
Restrained, curious, low-key determination. Think a single instrument (piano or guitar) with light rhythm — this is the "why would he do that" section, so hold back the full score.

Phase 2 (1:30 to about 3:00 — the track stats and the Lincoln decision):
Build steadily. By the time Charlie says the three track times (10.89, 21.67, 46.14), the music should be rising into a fuller arrangement — this is the "wait, this guy is actually elite" reveal.

Phase 3 (3:00 to end — Lincoln debut, the thesis, the CTA):
Full, motivational, triumphant swell — this should feel like a highlight-reel payoff, not a bittersweet one. This story has an unambiguously positive framing, so let the music actually feel good.

WHERE TO FIND ROYALTY-FREE MUSIC:
YouTube Audio Library, Pixabay, Artlist, or Epidemic Sound.
Search terms: "motivational underdog instrumental," "inspiring sports build," "triumphant indie sports score."

STEP 1 — Import music:
Ctrl+I, select your music file, Open. It lands in the Music bin.

STEP 2 — Place music in the Timeline:
Drag to Audio Track 3 (A3), below Charlie's voice (A1).

STEP 3 — Lower the music volume:
Select the music clip. Open Window > Effect Controls. Under Volume > Level, type -22 and press Enter. Adjust between -18dB and -25dB based on the track — if you can clearly hear every word, it's right.

STEP 4 — Fade out at the end:
Right-click the music clip > Show Clip Keyframes > Volume > Level. Click the yellow line about 10 seconds before the end (adds a keyframe), then click at the very last frame and drag that keyframe down to the bottom.

== SECTION G: COLOR GRADING ==

OPENING LUMETRI COLOR:
Window > Lumetri Color.

STEP 1 — Grade the talking head clips:
Select a talking head clip. Basic Correction:
  Exposure: +0.2
  Contrast: +15
  Highlights: -10
  Shadows: +10
  Whites: +5
  Blacks: -5

COLOR TREATMENT RECOMMENDATION FOR THIS VIDEO:
This is an optimistic, triumphant story — brighter and warmer than a heavy documentary grade, not moody or desaturated.

Color Wheels & Match: push Midtones slightly toward warm amber. Skip the cool-shadow push used on heavier stories — keep shadows neutral to keep the overall feel bright and hopeful.

Curves: a gentle S-curve is fine, but keep it subtle — this story doesn't need heavy contrast to feel serious, it needs to feel clean and energetic.

STEP 2 — Copy the grade to all talking head clips:
Right-click the graded clip > Copy. Select all other talking head clips (Shift+click) > right-click > Paste Attributes > check Lumetri Color > OK.

FOR B-ROLL/ARCHIVAL PHOTOS:
Lumetri Color > Creative: reduce Vibrance to -10, Saturation to -5, only on older/lower-quality archival images to keep them visually consistent.
