JOC COMMUNITY SPOTLIGHT POSTS
==============================
Format: Image + brief accolade breakdown. No quiz. No poll.
These go between the trivia posts to keep the feed educational.
Written to educate, inspire, and remind people why The JOC exists.


POST 1 — Deacon Jones
-----------------------
📸 Photo: Deacon Jones in Los Angeles Rams uniform

Deacon Jones. Mississippi Vocational College (now Mississippi Valley State).

He invented the word "sack." Before Deacon Jones, the NFL didn't even have a name for what he did to quarterbacks. He coined the term himself in the 1960s because he said tackling a QB behind the line of scrimmage was like a nuclear attack — a sack of a city.

He was selected to 8 Pro Bowls between 1964 and 1971. He was named to the NFL 100th Anniversary All-Time Team. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1980.

The man who defined one of football's most important stats came from an HBCU. And most people don't know his name.


POST 2 — Willie Lanier
-----------------------
📸 Photo: Willie Lanier in Kansas City Chiefs uniform

Willie Lanier. Morgan State University.

In 1967 he became the first Black player to start at middle linebacker in AFL history — one of the most physically demanding positions in football. He anchored the Kansas City Chiefs defense for 11 seasons, was selected to 8 Pro Bowls, and won Super Bowl IV in January 1970 when the Chiefs defeated the Minnesota Vikings 23-7.

He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1986 and named to the NFL 100th Anniversary All-Time Team.

Morgan State produced the man who broke a barrier at the most violent position on the field. That story never gets told enough.


POST 3 — Donnie Shell
-----------------------
📸 Photo: Donnie Shell in Pittsburgh Steelers uniform

Donnie Shell. South Carolina State University.

He went undrafted in 1974. The Pittsburgh Steelers signed him as a free agent and he became one of the most feared safeties in NFL history. Over 14 seasons he recorded 51 career interceptions and earned 5 Pro Bowl selections.

He won four Super Bowl rings — Super Bowl IX in January 1975, Super Bowl X in January 1976, Super Bowl XIII in January 1979, and Super Bowl XIV in January 1980. In 2020 he was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame.

Four rings. Undrafted. From South Carolina State. HBCU built that.


POST 4 — Rayfield Wright
-----------------------
📸 Photo: Rayfield Wright in Dallas Cowboys uniform

Rayfield Wright. Fort Valley State University.

They called him "The Big Cat." From 1967 to 1979 he protected Roger Staubach's blind side for the Dallas Cowboys and became one of the most dominant offensive tackles in NFL history. He was selected to 6 Pro Bowls and appeared in five Super Bowls, winning Super Bowl VI in January 1972 and Super Bowl XII in January 1978.

He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2006.

Fort Valley State. A D2 HBCU in Georgia. That's where The Big Cat came from.


POST 5 — Lem Barney
-----------------------
📸 Photo: Lem Barney in Detroit Lions uniform

Lem Barney. Jackson State University.

In his very first NFL season in 1967 he intercepted 10 passes and returned 3 of them for touchdowns. He was named NFL Defensive Rookie of the Year. Over 11 seasons with the Detroit Lions he recorded 56 career interceptions and earned 7 Pro Bowl selections.

He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1992.

Jackson State has produced some of the most dangerous defensive backs in NFL history. Lem Barney is one of the reasons why.


POST 6 — Willie Brown
-----------------------
📸 Photo: Willie Brown in Oakland Raiders uniform

Willie Brown. Grambling State University.

He played 16 seasons in professional football — first with the Denver Broncos and then with the Oakland Raiders — and became one of the most feared cornerbacks of his generation. He was selected to 9 Pro Bowls and in Super Bowl XI in January 1977 he intercepted a pass and returned it 75 yards for a touchdown to seal the Raiders' championship.

He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1984.

Grambling State. Super Bowl champion. Hall of Famer. The lineage runs deep.


POST 7 — Mel Blount
-----------------------
📸 Photo: Mel Blount in Pittsburgh Steelers uniform

Mel Blount. Southern University.

He was so dominant at cornerback that the NFL changed its rules because of him. In 1978 the league introduced what became known as the "Mel Blount Rule" — limiting how long defenders could make contact with receivers — because nobody could stop what he was doing to opposing offenses.

He played 14 seasons for the Pittsburgh Steelers, won four Super Bowl rings, earned 5 Pro Bowl selections, and was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1989.

Southern University produced a player so good the NFL rewrote the rulebook. Let that sink in.


POST 8 — Ed "Too Tall" Jones
-----------------------
📸 Photo: Ed "Too Tall" Jones in Dallas Cowboys uniform

Ed "Too Tall" Jones. Tennessee State University.

At 6'9 he was one of the most physically imposing players in NFL history. The Dallas Cowboys drafted him 1st overall in 1974 making him one of the first HBCU players ever taken at that position. He played 15 seasons in Dallas, earned 3 Pro Bowl selections, and appeared in three Super Bowls — winning Super Bowl XII in January 1978.

Tennessee State didn't just produce a great player. They produced the first overall pick.


POST 9 — James "Shack" Harris
-----------------------
📸 Photo: James Harris in Los Angeles Rams uniform

James "Shack" Harris. Grambling State University.

In 1974 he became the first Black quarterback to start a full NFL season and lead his team to the playoffs — doing it with the Los Angeles Rams. The following year he was named to the Pro Bowl, becoming one of the first Black quarterbacks ever selected to the game.

He did this in an era when the quarterback position was considered off-limits to Black players at the professional level. He kicked that door open.

Doug Williams got the Super Bowl. Shack Harris opened the door that made it possible. Grambling State built them both.


POST 10 — Elvin Bethea
-----------------------
📸 Photo: Elvin Bethea in Houston Oilers uniform

Elvin Bethea. North Carolina A&T State University.

For 16 seasons from 1968 to 1983 he was one of the most disruptive defensive ends in the NFL. He earned 8 Pro Bowl selections playing for the Houston Oilers and was named to the NFL 1970s All-Decade Team.

In 2003 he was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame — one of the most underrecognized players ever to play the position.

North Carolina A&T. 8 Pro Bowls. Hall of Fame. The HBCU pipeline to professional football has always been real. The spotlight just never followed.
