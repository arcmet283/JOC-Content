POST 1 — Robert Brazile | Jackson State University
----------------------------------------------------
📸 Photo: Robert Brazile in Houston Oilers uniform

Robert Brazile. Jackson State University.

They called him "Dr. Doom." A consensus All-American at Jackson State, he was taken 6th overall in the first round of the 1975 NFL Draft by the Houston Oilers — one of the highest picks an HBCU player had ever commanded. He didn't waste the opportunity. In his rookie season he was named NFL Defensive Rookie of the Year, and from 1976 to 1982 he was selected to seven straight Pro Bowls at linebacker.

Over 10 seasons in Houston he started all 147 regular season games he played, made back-to-back AFC Championship Game appearances in 1978 and 1979, and built a reputation as one of the most feared linebackers of his era. In 2018 he was finally inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame — the fourth Jackson State Tiger enshrined in Canton.

A 6th overall pick. Seven Pro Bowls. A gold jacket. Jackson State built Dr. Doom, and the league felt it for a decade.


POST 2 — Leroy Kelly | Morgan State University
--------------------------------------------------
📸 Photo: Leroy Kelly in Cleveland Browns uniform

Leroy Kelly. Morgan State University.

He spent his first two NFL seasons doing what almost nobody could — sitting behind Jim Brown and waiting his turn. When Brown retired before the 1966 season, Kelly stepped in and made the league forget the position had ever been in question. In 1967 and 1968 he led the entire NFL in rushing both years, and he topped the league in rushing touchdowns three straight seasons from 1966 to 1968.

He finished his career with 7,274 rushing yards and 74 rushing touchdowns, added another 2,281 receiving yards, and racked up over 12,000 all-purpose yards. He earned six Pro Bowl selections, five All-NFL honors, and won the Bert Bell Award as the league's top player in 1968. In 1994 he was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame.

Morgan State handed the NFL a running back good enough to replace a legend — and he didn't just fill the void, he owned it.
