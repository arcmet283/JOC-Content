POST 1 — Art Shell | Maryland State College (now University of Maryland Eastern Shore)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
📸 Photo: Art Shell in Oakland Raiders uniform

Art Shell. Maryland State College (now University of Maryland Eastern Shore).

The Oakland Raiders drafted him in the 3rd round of the 1968 NFL Draft, 80th overall, and he became the anchor of one of the most dominant offensive lines in football history. He was selected to 8 Pro Bowls, earned 2 First-Team All-Pro selections, and helped power the Raiders to Super Bowl XI in January 1977 and Super Bowl XV in January 1981 — winning both. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1989.

That same year, the Raiders named him head coach — making him the first Black head coach in the modern NFL era. He didn't just protect the quarterback. He broke down a door that had stayed shut for decades.

Maryland State College. A small HBCU on the Eastern Shore. It produced a Hall of Famer and a history-making head coach in the same man.


POST 2 — Charlie Joiner | Grambling State University
------------------------------------------------------
📸 Photo: Charlie Joiner in San Diego Chargers uniform

Charlie Joiner. Grambling State University.

He played under legendary coach Eddie Robinson at Grambling, catching passes from fellow future pro James "Shack" Harris. The Houston Oilers drafted him in the 4th round of the 1969 AFL Draft. Over 18 NFL seasons with the Oilers, Cincinnati Bengals, and San Diego Chargers, he built one of the most durable careers the position has ever seen.

When he retired after the 1986 season at age 39, he walked away as the NFL's all-time leader in both career receptions (750) and receiving yards (12,146) — the most productive wide receiver the league had ever seen. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1996.

Grambling State didn't just send a receiver to the NFL. It sent the record book's new owner.
