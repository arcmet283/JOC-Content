So, what if I told you one of the most efficient running backs in the NFL over the last two seasons didn't come from Alabama, didn't come from Ohio State, didn't come from any FBS program at all — and wasn't even drafted? Well, that's Emanuel Wilson. And if you don't know his name yet, that needs to change.

Fort Valley State. If you don't know the name, it's a historically Black university in Fort Valley, Georgia, competing at the Division II level. The town has a population of about 7,000 people. There are no primetime games, no ESPN cameras, no five-star recruiting classes showing up on signing day. What Fort Valley State does have is a football program that takes players the scouting world ignores and develops them into something real. Emanuel Wilson is the clearest proof of that.

In two seasons at Fort Valley State, Wilson ran for over 2,200 yards and 24 touchdowns. In 2022, he put up 1,371 rushing yards and 17 touchdowns and was named the SIAC Offensive Player of the Year. Not just the best running back in his conference — the best offensive player. Period. At the D2 level. Against guys nobody was scouting. And when the 2023 NFL Draft came around, all 259 picks went by and not one team called his name.

And here's where most players pack it up.

Emanuel Wilson signed with the Green Bay Packers as an undrafted free agent. Most undrafted free agents don't make the roster. The percentage who even see a regular season snap is small. Wilson made the team, worked his way up the depth chart, and when his number was called, he did not waste the opportunity.

In 2024, he rushed for 502 yards and four touchdowns on 103 carries, averaging 4.87 yards per attempt. That ranked eighth in the entire NFL among running backs with at least 100 carries. Eighth. In the NFL. Out of Fort Valley State. Undrafted. The Packers finished 11-6 that season. Wilson was part of that run. Over three seasons in Green Bay, he totaled 1,083 rushing yards and seven touchdowns — and in 2025, the Seattle Seahawks saw enough and signed him as a free agent.

Now I want you to sit with something. Because this story has a blueprint. In 1985, a wide receiver out of Mississippi Valley State — another HBCU, another SWAC program that the scouting world had largely ignored — went 16th overall to the San Francisco 49ers. His name was Jerry Rice. And by the time he was done, there was no debate about who the greatest receiver who ever played the game was. He ended that conversation by himself.

Now the scouts almost missed him too. He played at Mississippi Valley State against competition that nobody was watching closely. But the 49ers took a look and saw what was real. Emanuel Wilson isn't Jerry Rice. But the pattern is identical. An HBCU player from a program the mainstream had written off, who got to the NFL and proved that the only thing the scouts had wrong was the school name on the helmet.

Wilson has always been straightforward about where he came from and what it meant. Talent is talent. It doesn't matter what level you play at, what conference you compete in, or whether anybody put a star next to your name at 17 years old. If you can play, you can play. And the football itself has been proving that as long as the game has existed.

But here's what gets me. If Emanuel Wilson had gone to a Power 4 school out of high school, he would've been a first-round pick. The production is undeniable. The efficiency is undeniable. The only variable that changed is the logo on the helmet. And there are players like Wilson at Grambling, at Southern, at Fort Valley State every single year who will never get the look they deserve simply because of where they play.

He didn't let that stop him. He signed an undrafted deal, made a roster, earned a role, put up top-ten numbers in the entire NFL, and earned a contract with a franchise that's building something real in Seattle. If you slept on his name before, there's no excuse now.
