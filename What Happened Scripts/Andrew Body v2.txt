So, what if I told you that for the first time since Tarvaris Jackson suited up for Alabama State, a Hornets quarterback has a legitimate shot at the NFL Draft? And what if I told you that same quarterback had offers from Notre Dame, Penn State, and Florida — and turned every single one of them down to come back and finish what he started? Well, that's Andrew Body. And this story has layers.

It all started at Seffner Christian Academy in Texas, where Andrew was already rewriting the record books before he ever stepped on a college field. Over 13,000 career passing yards. 91 touchdowns. Back-to-back 4,000-yard seasons. Named District MVP, All-State, and a finalist for Mr. Texas Football. And by the time he graduated, he ranked third all-time in Texas high school passing history. Third. All-time. In Texas. And with numbers like that, you would think the Power 5 schools were lined up at his door. But Andrew made a different choice. He committed to Texas Southern.

And right away, he made his presence felt. As a freshman in 2021, he set the single-season total offense record at TSU, averaging nearly 266 yards per game. The flashes were undeniable. The talent was real. And you couldn't tell anyone this kid wasn't going to be a star.

But it wasn't all smooth sailing. Injuries crept in, including a shoulder issue that slowed him down. And when the school moved on from head coach Clarence McKinney after the 2023 season, Andrew started looking for a new home. He wanted a coach who could take him to the next level. So he transferred to Alabama State.

His debut in a Hornets uniform was exactly what everyone expected — 134 rushing yards, two touchdowns on the ground, another through the air. And then that same shoulder shut him down again. Season over. And at that point, most people would have packed it up and headed to the portal for good.

But Andrew Body came back.

The 2025 season arrives and he is not playing like a guy who has been through two programs and two shoulder injuries. He completed over 70% of his passes for 1,770 yards, 20 touchdowns, and just one interception. He added 518 rushing yards and four more scores on the ground. And he did all of that in just eight games before that shoulder flared up again and shut him down for the final four. Eight games. 20 touchdowns. One interception. That's not just good — that's historic.

And the awards followed. SWAC Offensive Player of the Year. Stats Perform HBCU National Player of the Year. BOXTOROW National Offensive Player of the Year. And on top of all that, he led Alabama State to their first 10-win season since 2004.

Now if that year sounds familiar, it should. Because 2004 was the last time Alabama State had a quarterback this special. That quarterback was Tarvaris Jackson. And after leading the Hornets to that same 10-win milestone, Jackson went on to become the last quarterback ever drafted out of Alabama State — taken in the second round by the Minnesota Vikings in 2006. He started in Minnesota. He started in Seattle. He played in this league for a decade. He built that entire career on what he proved at an HBCU. And that was nearly 20 years ago. Since then, no Alabama State quarterback has come close to that stage.

Until now.

And then he pulled his name out of the portal and came back to Alabama State.

Here's why that matters. If Andrew Body had transferred to Florida or Penn State, he would've been chasing a dream on someone else's terms — starting over in a new system, with one year to prove himself to coaches who didn't recruit him. But by coming back to Alabama State, he controls the narrative. He's the guy. The system is built around him. And if he has the season everyone expects, he won't just be another HBCU quarterback hoping to get noticed — he'll be the player that forces the NFL to come to him.

The NFL hasn't drafted a quarterback out of Alabama State since Tarvaris Jackson in 2006. That's a chapter that's been sitting open for two decades. Andrew Body came back to close it. And he chose to do it in Hornet colors.

Talent opens the door. But coming back when you didn't have to — that's what makes legends.
