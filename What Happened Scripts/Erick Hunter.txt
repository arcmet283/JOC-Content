So, what if I told you the fastest linebacker at the NFL Combine — a man who met with 31 of the 32 NFL franchises, ran a 4.48-second forty, jumped 37 inches off the ground, and led his entire conference in tackles — couldn't get his name called on draft day?

Pay attention to this one.

Because this is not just one man's story. This is a story about a system. And Erick Hunter from Morgan State University is the most important HBCU athlete in recent memory for one painful reason: he did everything right. Everything.

Morgan State is a Baltimore institution. A school that has been producing real football players for generations. And when Erick Hunter arrived on that campus, he became one of the most disruptive defenders in the MEAC from almost the moment he got there. Not a project. Not somebody who needed years to develop. He showed up and made his presence felt immediately.

The numbers built over four years. Nearly 300 career tackles — 298, to be exact. That puts him second on Morgan State's all-time tackles list. Second. All-time. At a school that has been playing football for over a century.

And if you know your HBCU football history, you know what that means. Because Morgan State is the same school that produced Willie Lanier — one of the most feared middle linebackers in the history of the NFL. Hall of Famer. Super Bowl champion. A man who came out of Baltimore and showed the entire league what a linebacker from an HBCU could do. Lanier anchored the Kansas City Chiefs defense for a decade. He was the first Black player to start at middle linebacker in the AFL. And he built that career on what he proved at Morgan State. Erick Hunter walks those same sidelines. That is not a coincidence. That is a tradition.

And then came his final season. He goes out and leads the entire MEAC with 102 tackles. He adds 14 tackles for loss. Four sacks. Four forced fumbles. An interception. And one of those moments that makes the highlight reel for years — he returns a blocked kick 90 yards for a touchdown against Norfolk State. A linebacker. Running 90 yards like he had somewhere important to be.

The national voters noticed. FCS Second-Team All-America. A finalist for the Buck Buchanan Award — the highest individual honor in FCS football for a defensive player. He was not just an HBCU standout. He was a national standout.

And then the Combine happens.

He runs a 4.48 forty. You know what that number means? That made him the third fastest linebacker in the entire draft class. Not the third fastest HBCU linebacker. Third fastest out of every linebacker in the country. Period. He jumps 37 inches. He is an elite athlete by any measure — HBCU, FBS, Power Four, none of that matters when you run a 4.48.

And the NFL knew it. Because Erick Hunter met with 31 of the 32 NFL franchises during the pre-draft process. Thirty-one teams flew him in, sat across a table from him, broke down his film, and asked him about coverages, tendencies, game situations. Thirty-one organizations said yes — we want to know this man.

So draft day comes.

Seven rounds. Two hundred and fifty-seven picks.

Erick Hunter's name was never called.

And here is the part that makes this bigger than one draft class. The year before, Carson Vinson from Alabama A&M was the only HBCU player selected — one pick out of 257. And in this draft, after Erick Hunter runs the third fastest linebacker forty at the combine, meets with 31 teams, and earns national All-America recognition — zero again.

Think about the history of what we are really talking about. Willie Lanier came out of Morgan State and became a Hall of Famer. Jerry Rice came out of Mississippi Valley State and became the greatest receiver the game has ever seen. Deacon Jones came out of an HBCU and reinvented what it meant to rush the passer. These schools have always produced elite talent. And there has always been a credibility gap somewhere in the evaluation process between a player coming out of Morgan State and a player coming out of a Power Four school — even when the Morgan State guy is running faster than everyone else in the room.

That is the thing that makes Erick Hunter's story impossible to look away from. He left no doubt. There was no argument to be made on athletic merit. And they still passed.

Now here is where things stand. The Detroit Lions came calling after the draft ended. They signed him as an undrafted free agent. And one Lions analyst put it plainly — Erick Hunter is more athletic and a better player than Alex Anzalone was when he entered the league. Anzalone went on to become a reliable starter for Detroit. That comparison is not an accident. That is a scout telling you exactly what this signing could become.

Undrafted free agents earn roster spots every single season. There is a real path here. But the question this story forces all of us to sit with is this: why was the draft necessary? Why did 31 organizations evaluate this man at an elite level and then collectively pass? What is actually happening inside those draft rooms that keeps HBCU talent on the outside — even when the measurables say he belongs?

Because Erick Hunter is not the exception. He is part of a pattern. And patterns do not fix themselves.
