So, what if I told you the fastest linebacker at the NFL Combine — a man who met with 31 of the 32 NFL franchises, ran a 4.48-second forty, jumped 37 inches off the ground, and led his entire conference in tackles — couldn't get his name called on draft day?

And I need you to pay attention to this one.

Because this is not just one man's story. This is a story about a system. And Erick Hunter from Morgan State University is the most important HBCU athlete in recent memory for one painful reason: he did everything right.

Everything.

Morgan State is a Baltimore institution. A school that has been producing real football players for generations. And when Erick Hunter walked onto that campus, he became one of the most disruptive defenders in the MEAC from almost the moment he arrived. Not somebody who needed years to develop. Not a project. He showed up and made his presence felt right away.

And as his career built, the numbers told the story. Over four years, he racked up nearly 300 career tackles. Two hundred and ninety-eight. That puts him second on Morgan State's all-time tackles list. Second. All-time. At a school that has been playing football for over a century.

And if you know your HBCU football history, you know what that means. Because Morgan State is the same school that produced Willie Lanier — one of the most feared linebackers in the history of the NFL. A Hall of Famer. A Super Bowl champion. A man who came out of Baltimore and showed the whole league what a linebacker from an HBCU could do. Erick Hunter walks those same sidelines. That is not a coincidence. That is a tradition.

And then came his final season. His last run in a Morgan State uniform. He goes out and leads the entire MEAC — the whole conference — with 102 tackles. He adds 14 tackles for loss. Four sacks. Four forced fumbles. An interception. And one of those moments that makes the highlight reel for years — he returns a blocked kick 90 yards for a touchdown against Norfolk State. A linebacker. Running 90 yards. Like he had somewhere important to be.

The national voters took notice. He earns FCS Second-Team All-America honors. He becomes a finalist for the Buck Buchanan Award — the highest individual honor in FCS football for a defensive player. He is not just an HBCU standout at that point. He is a national standout.

And then the NFL Combine happens.

He runs a 4.48 forty. You know what that number means? That made him the third fastest linebacker in the entire draft class. Not the third fastest HBCU linebacker. Third fastest. Out of every linebacker in the country. Period. And he jumps 37 inches. The man is an elite athlete by any measure — HBCU, FBS, Power Four, none of that matters when you run a 4.48.

And the NFL knew it. Because Erick Hunter met with 31 of the 32 NFL franchises during the pre-draft process. Thirty-one teams. They flew him in. Sat across a table from him. Broke down his film. Asked him about coverages, tendencies, game situations. And thirty-one organizations said yes — we want to know this man.

So draft day comes.

You'd think the phone is ringing.

Seven rounds. Two hundred and fifty-seven picks.

And Erick Hunter's name was never called.

Not once.

And here is the part that makes this bigger than one draft class. This was the second time in three years that not a single HBCU player was selected in the NFL Draft. Zero. The year before, Carson Vinson from Alabama A&M got picked up by the Ravens — just one player, but at least it was something. Before that, nothing. And now, after Erick Hunter runs the third fastest linebacker forty at the combine, meets with 31 teams, earns national All-America recognition — nothing again.

These schools have always produced elite talent. And there has always been a barrier. A credibility gap that exists somewhere in the evaluation process between a player coming out of Morgan State and a player coming out of a Power Four program — even when the Morgan State guy is running faster than everyone else in the room.

That is the thing that makes Erick Hunter's story impossible to look away from. He did not leave any doubt. There was no argument to be made on pure athletic merit. He was third fastest.
