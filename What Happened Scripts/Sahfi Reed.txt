He had offers from Syracuse. UConn. Temple. Akron. Old Dominion. Instead, he walked on. At a Power Four school. For a chance to barely see the field. And it still wasn't the wrong decision — because what happened next nobody saw coming.

Sahfi Reed grew up in Philadelphia, a two-sport star at Martin Luther King High School — a wide receiver and defensive back on the football field, and an All-American caliber sprinter on the track. And when it came time to pick a college, he didn't take the easy road. He had real Division I football offers on the table. He turned them down and walked on at the University of Houston instead — for both football and track — betting on himself to earn a scholarship the hard way at a Power Four program.

On the football field, that bet didn't pay off. Reed was buried on Houston's depth chart, limited to spot duty at wide receiver, never getting the reps to show what he could do. But on the track, it was a completely different story.

Running the 4x400 relay for Houston, Reed became a USTFCCCA Second Team All-American in 2025. His numbers are absurd for a football player: 10.89 in the 100 meters. 21.67 in the 200. 46.14 in the 400. That's not "fast for a receiver." That's elite, verified, track-program speed. And it still wasn't enough to get him real touches on Saturdays.

So in January 2026, Sahfi Reed made a decision that flips the entire transfer portal script. He didn't jump from Houston to another Power Four school chasing a bigger name. He committed to Lincoln University in Pennsylvania — a Division II HBCU with one of the most decorated track programs in the country, 17 NCAA titles. He walked away from a Power Four roster spot to go be a two-sport star at an HBCU that could actually use everything he had.

At Lincoln, Reed arrived with three years of football eligibility left and track speed that no CIAA program — or most Division I programs — can match. He didn't waste time proving it. He's already posted a 21.85 in the indoor 200 meters as a Lion, right in line with the times that made him an All-American at Houston. And in the 2026 season, wearing number 10 for Lincoln, Sahfi Reed finally made his HBCU football debut — the first time since high school he's stepped on a football field as more than an afterthought.

Here's why this story matters. Every week, the portal is full of players trying to climb the ladder — small school to big school, chasing exposure, chasing NIL money, chasing a bigger stage. Sahfi Reed did the opposite. He had the Power Four roster spot. He gave it up. Not because he failed — because a Division II HBCU actually offered him something Houston never did: a real chance to be great at both of the things he's great at.

Where a lot of players would've stayed buried on a Power Four bench just to say they're "Division I," Reed bet on himself twice — once walking on at Houston, and again walking away from it. That's not a fallback plan. That's a stat major HBCU football need to see get told.
