So, what if I told you one of the best comeback stories in professional football started at Howard University — and ended with an MVP trophy and an NFL contract? Well, that's Ian Wheeler. And the road to get there was not straight.

Howard University is not the first place you think of when you talk about NFL talent. It's one of the most prestigious HBCUs in the country — a school that has produced Thurgood Marshall, Toni Morrison, and some of the most influential people in American history. But professional football? That's not the conversation people usually have about Howard. Ian Wheeler changed that.

Wheeler spent five seasons with the Howard Bison, rushing for over 1,100 yards and 14 touchdowns. He wasn't on anybody's draft board. But he was productive, he was tough, and he believed he could play at the next level. When the NFL Draft passed without his name being called, he signed with the Chicago Bears as an undrafted free agent.

And then the worst thing that can happen to a player trying to make a roster happened. During the preseason, Wheeler tore his ACL. Season over before it started. The Bears eventually released him with an injury settlement. And at that point, most people would have moved on. An HBCU background, an ACL tear, released by an NFL team — the odds were stacking up against him.

But Ian Wheeler kept going.

He found his way to the Louisville Kings of the United Football League. And if you're not familiar with the UFL, it's a professional league that gives players a real shot — guys who didn't make it through the NFL process but can still play football at a high level. Wheeler didn't just make the team. He became one of their best players.

And then came the UFL Championship Game.

Louisville Kings versus the DC Defenders. At Audi Field in Washington, D.C. — practically Howard's backyard. Ian Wheeler went out there in front of a national audience and ran for 81 yards and a touchdown. The Kings won the championship 27-20. And when it was over, they handed Ian Wheeler the United Bowl MVP trophy.

Now I need you to understand what that name means and the company it puts him in.

Richard Dent played college football at Tennessee State University — an HBCU. He went undrafted in 1983, signed with the Chicago Bears, and won Super Bowl XX MVP against the New England Patriots. He was drafted in the first round, and when he got his Super Bowl start with Washington, he threw four touchdown passes in the second quarter alone and won MVP against one of the best defenses in the NFL. Jerry Rice played college football at Mississippi Valley State University — an HBCU. He went 16th overall to San Francisco and became the greatest receiver who ever played the game. He won three Super Bowls and his Super Bowl XXIII MVP performance is still one of the finest in the history of the sport.
