TOP STORY THIS WEEK:
Morgan State Bears Football — Baltimore, MD (MEAC / FCS)
Morgan State entered the Stats Perform FCS Top 25 on October 12, 2026 at No. 24 — the first time in history any HBCU has appeared in the national media poll. The Bears are 5-1 after a 28-27 homecoming thriller over Robert Morris, their best six-game start since 2009. Starting QB Cameron Edge (formerly Maryland/ACC, then Eastern Michigan/MAC) threw for 444 yards against Villanova — third all-time in program history. But the current run is rooted in a dynasty most fans have forgotten: Hall of Fame coach Earl "Papa Bear" Banks (1960–1973) compiled a 96-31-2 record, ran a 31-game win streak (1964–1968), won three consecutive undefeated regular seasons (1965–67), produced NFL stars Willie Lanier, Leroy Kelly, and Raymond Chester, and won the 1966 Tangerine Bowl and 1965 Orange Blossom Classic. Before Banks, head coach Edward P. Hurt (1924–1960) ran a 54-game win streak from 1931–1938 — widely recognized as the longest in college football at the time. Morgan State honored both coaches with a Legends Plaza dedication in 2017 and celebrated Banks' full legacy at homecoming 2026. Head coach Damon Wilson (who previously built the Bowie State championship program) is leading the current revival. The arc — dynasty, dormancy, revival, and now history — fits the JOC "What Happened" format exactly.
Hook angle: "This week, Morgan State became the first HBCU in history to make the national FCS Top 25. But this is not the first time the Bears were the best team in Black college football — and the story of why anyone forgot about them is where the real video lives."
Audience this serves: 35-54 (Earl Banks era, classic NFL stars like Lanier and Kelly); 25-44 (current FCS ranking milestone, ongoing season narrative)

4. "He Was the Most Feared Linebacker in the AFL — Nobody Knew He Went to Morgan State" — Willie Lanier / Classic Era (Pre-2000)
Story: Willie Lanier was a two-time All-American linebacker at Morgan State under Earl Banks (1964–1966), then became the first Black player to start at middle linebacker in the AFL/NFL when he joined the Kansas City Chiefs. Nicknamed "Contact" for his punishing physicality, he was a key member of the Chiefs' Super Bowl IV championship team (1970) and two AFL championships. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1986 and is considered one of the greatest defensive players in football history. His Morgan State connection is almost completely unknown outside Baltimore — making him a perfect "I had no idea" hook. Morgan State's current homecoming celebration of the Banks era makes this story freshly timely this week.
Why this works for JOC: Classic era / "you didn't know this" format; Kansas City Chiefs and Super Bowl IV search traffic; directly connects to the Top Story's Morgan State program arc; serves the 35-54 audience that lived through the AFL era.
