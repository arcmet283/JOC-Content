TOP STORY THIS WEEK:
Bethune-Cookman Wildcats — SWAC (Daytona Beach, FL)
In Week 8 of the 2026 season (October 25), Bethune-Cookman University defeated previously top-ranked Jackson State 24-21 at home — the Wildcats' first win over the Tigers since joining the SWAC, and the single biggest upset in HBCU football this week. Jackson State had a final chance to force a tie but missed a 49-yard field goal as time expired. The result reshuffled the entire SWAC East and sent Alabama A&M to the top of the Black College Football Poll. The win doesn't exist in isolation: it is the culmination of a four-year rebuild by head coach Raymond Woodie Jr. that started with a 2-10 season in 2024, produced a 6-6 record and a dramatic 38-34 win over rival Florida A&M in the 2025 Florida Classic, and has now climbed to beating the conference's most celebrated program. The connective tissue between B-CU's history and this moment is Nick Collins — the school's most famous football alum. Collins was a 2002-2004 All-MEAC safety and three-year starter at B-CU, was drafted 51st overall by the Green Bay Packers in 2005 (the highest HBCU draft pick in that cycle), and his Super Bowl XLV interception return for a touchdown against Ben Roethlisberger (Packers 31, Steelers 25) is one of the most iconic plays in Super Bowl history. After his 2011 neck injury ended his playing career, Collins returned to B-CU as cornerbacks coach from 2023 to 2025 — a direct investment in the same program that made him. On January 15, 2026, Collins was elected to the Black College Football Hall of Fame. Now, four months after his induction, the program he helped build is beating Jackson State for the first time in program history while in the SWAC.
Hook angle: "The school that produced the Super Bowl XLV hero — and then brought him back as a coach — just knocked the SWAC's most celebrated program out of the unbeaten ranks. This is the Bethune-Cookman story nobody has told."
Audience this serves: 35-54 (Nick Collins/Super Bowl XLV nostalgia), 25-44 (current SWAC season), 18-34 (current generation of HBCU fans)
Title format suggestion: Program milestone / Classic Era — "What Happened to Bethune-Cookman Football?" or "They Sent a Player to the Super Bowl. Then Everything Fell Apart. Now They're Back." or "The HBCU That Produced a Super Bowl Hero Just Shocked the Entire SWAC."

3. "He Left His HBCU — Then Led All NFL Rookies in Rushing Touchdowns" — Jacory Croskey-Merritt [Draft / Advocacy — Current Era]
Story: Jacory Croskey-Merritt began his college career at Alabama State, where he rushed for more than 1,100 yards and 13 touchdowns in one season — production that drew FBS attention. He transferred to New Mexico, then to Arizona, and the Washington Commanders selected him 245th overall in the 2025 NFL Draft. As a rookie in 2025, he led all NFL rookies in rushing touchdowns with eight — one of the more remarkable outputs by a late-round pick in recent memory. He enters 2026 on injured reserve with 1,044 career rushing yards and 10 touchdowns. His path — leave your HBCU to get drafted, succeed in the NFL — is both an individual triumph and a structural indictment. The three players drafted from HBCU programs in 2026 (Kevin Coleman, Karon Prunty, Eric Fuller) all had to transfer to FBS schools first; Croskey-Merritt's 2025 draft class was the same story.
Why this works for JOC: A hopeful counter-narrative to the draft shutout story — an HBCU product who survived the transfer, made the NFL, and performed at the top of the league's rookie class. The "left his HBCU to get drafted" framing ties it to advocacy without being only about failure; it's about what it costs and what it can produce.

Idea A: "101 Years of the Game Nobody Outside Texas Knows About: The State Fair Classic"
Angle: The 101st annual State Fair Classic was played September 26, 2026 at the Cotton Bowl in Dallas — Prairie View A&M vs. Grambling State, two programs that have met in the shadow of the Texas State Fair for over a century. This game predates the racial integration of major Texas college football. When UT and Texas A&M wouldn't let Black players suit up, the State Fair Classic in Dallas was where Black college football lived. The Cotton Bowl holds 92,000 fans; Dallas' sports commission actively promotes it as a civic institution; it generates tens of millions in economic activity — and still almost no one outside Texas knows its name.
Search hook: "State Fair Classic history," "Prairie View vs Grambling," "oldest HBCU football game Texas," "Cotton Bowl HBCU," "101st State Fair Classic"
Audience: 35-54 (historical depth), 25-44 (cultural pride, Texas football audience), also captures general Texas football fans searching Cotton Bowl history
