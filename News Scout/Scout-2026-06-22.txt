TOP STORY THIS WEEK:
Erick Hunter -- Morgan State University

For the second time in three years, not a single HBCU football player was selected in the 2026 NFL Draft -- and the story of HOW that happened is the most compelling HBCU sports narrative of 2026. Morgan State linebacker Erick Hunter was the undisputed top HBCU prospect in this draft class: a 6'2", 224-lb defender who ran a 4.48-second 40-yard dash (3rd fastest among all linebackers at the combine), posted a 37-inch vertical, and finished his senior season leading the MEAC with 102 total tackles, 14 tackles for loss, 4 sacks, 4 forced fumbles, and an interception. He ended his Morgan State career 2nd on the all-time tackles list with 298 career stops, earned FCS Second-Team All-America honors, was a Buck Buchanan Award finalist, and met with 31 of the NFL's 32 franchises during the pre-draft process. He also produced one of the highlight moments of the MEAC season -- a blocked-kick return for a 90-yard touchdown against Norfolk State. Despite all of it, his name was never called. Only Carson Vinson (Alabama A&M, 2025 Ravens) had been drafted from an HBCU in the prior two years. Hunter ultimately signed with the Detroit Lions as an undrafted free agent on April 25, 2026. His story is at once a deeply personal underdog journey and a systemic indictment of how NFL scouts evaluate talent from HBCU programs -- the exact combination that makes a great JOC "What Happened" video.

Hook angle: "What if I told you the fastest linebacker at this year's NFL Showcase -- a man who met with 31 of 32 NFL teams, ran a 4.48 forty, and left everything on the field at Morgan State -- couldn't get his name called on draft day?"

2. "Howard University Did Something No HBCU Has Done in Decades"
Story: The 2025-2026 academic year will go down as the greatest in Howard University athletics history. The men's basketball team, under head coach Kenneth Blakeney, captured its first-ever NCAA Tournament victory -- an 86-83 First Four win over UMBC on March 17 -- while the women's team received the highest seed (No. 14) of any Bison basketball program ever. Both teams won MEAC Tournament championships in the same year, a historic first. Beyond basketball, Howard captured 10 total conference championships across six sports including track and field, swimming, golf (men's and women's), and softball, making the 2025-26 cycle one of the most decorated seasons of any HBCU in recent memory. Three HBCU programs received NCAA Tournament bids in 2026 for the first time since 1994.
Why this works for JOC: It's a program-pride documentary waiting to happen -- multiple sports, a first-ever NCAA win, and a school that has been culturally prominent but athletically overlooked; perfect for the "leave viewers feeling educated and prideful" mission.

3. "The Night HBCU Baseball Came to Wrigley Field"
Story: On May 2, 2026 -- the 106th anniversary of the first Negro National League game -- Prairie View A&M and Alabama A&M played the first-ever HBCU baseball game at Wrigley Field in Chicago, as part of the inaugural Chicago HBCU Baseball Classic. Alabama A&M won 10-7, with first baseman Zack Rice earning MVP honors. The date was chosen deliberately: Wrigley Field is the only surviving ballpark where Jackie Robinson played, giving the event an almost sacred historical weight. Chicago Park District youth participants served as honorary managers in a symbolic passing-of-the-torch ceremony, and the event drew HBCU alumni, community leaders, and national media coverage.
Why this works for JOC: The layers of Black baseball history (Negro Leagues, Jackie Robinson, Wrigley) on top of a modern HBCU moment create the perfect documentary atmosphere -- visual, emotional, and historically rich.

4. "The Lost Powerhouse: Maryland State College Football and the Dynasty Nobody Remembers"
Story: Between 1946 and 1970, Maryland State College (now the University of Maryland Eastern Shore) compiled a 142-38 record, won seven undefeated seasons, and produced 30 professional football players including five Super Bowl participants -- a track record that rivaled any HBCU program in America. Head coach Vernon "Skip" McCain (hired from Tennessee State) built the program into a dominant independent force before it joined the CIAA, regularly beating conference rivals and generating legendary rivalries (particularly against Morgan State). The program produced collegiate and professional Hall of Famers, a civil rights activist, and an aide to Robert F. Kennedy. It has been almost entirely forgotten by mainstream HBCU football history.
Why this works for JOC: A pure "What Happened" story -- a genuine dynasty with no national recognition, decades of erasure, and a rich cast of characters that would surprise even the most dedicated HBCU fan.

5. "Can Michael Vick Save Norfolk State?"
Story: NFL legend Michael Vick went 1-11 in his debut season as Norfolk State head coach in 2025, with the program's only win coming on a last-second field goal against Division II Virginia State. Vick's first year was documented in a BET docu-series, "The Coach Vick Experience," which chronicled the highs and painful lows of learning to lead an HBCU program from scratch. Now in Year 2, Vick has brought in three new quarterbacks, has 100% participation in strength and conditioning, and has publicly vowed to "leave Norfolk State better than I found it." He has also begun speaking openly about the Rooney Rule and his own ambitions for an NFL head coaching job, signaling that Norfolk State is a proving ground -- not a retirement stop.
Why this works for JOC: The redemption arc framing (struggling but committed superstar trying to build something real at an HBCU) plays perfectly against the 1-11 backdrop, and Vick's NFL ambitions add a compelling subplot.
