TOP STORY THIS WEEK:
JOHN STALLWORTH - Alabama A&M University
John Stallworth grew up in Tuscaloosa, Alabama — the same city as the University of Alabama — but Alabama never recruited him. He enrolled at Alabama A&M, an HBCU about 90 miles north, where he initially played running back before convincing coaches to move him to wide receiver. When NFL scouts came to evaluate him before the 1974 draft, they watched him run the 40-yard dash on a wet track and were unimpressed. Pittsburgh took a chance in the 4th round (82nd overall) — the same 1974 draft class as Lynn Swann — and got one of the greatest wide receivers in NFL history. Stallworth played all 14 seasons with the Steelers, won four Super Bowls, scored touchdowns in eight consecutive playoff games (an NFL record at the time), and finished with 537 receptions for 8,723 yards and 63 TDs. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2002. After his career, he returned to Alabama A&M to found a scholarship program for students at the school that made him. He was also inducted into the Black College Football Hall of Fame in 2014.
Hook angle: He grew up in Tuscaloosa. Alabama was right there. They didn't want him. Alabama A&M took him. And he became better than any wide receiver Alabama ever produced.
Audience this serves: 35-54 (classic era, Super Bowl nostalgia, Pittsburgh Steelers search traffic), also 25-44 (HBCU draft advocacy)
Title format suggestion: Advocacy + Classic Era ("Alabama Didn't Want Him. Alabama A&M Made Him a Hall of Famer." or "The HBCU Wide Receiver Who Won 4 Super Bowls — After Alabama Ignored Him")

2. "His Scholarship Was Revoked for Protesting. He Became the Greatest Pass Rusher in NFL History." — Deacon Jones / South Carolina State & Mississippi Valley State (Classic Era — Pre-2000)
   Story: David "Deacon" Jones arrived at South Carolina State in 1958, where he became a standout defensive lineman. But the school revoked his scholarship after learning he had participated in a Civil Rights Movement protest. A departing assistant coach helped Jones land at Mississippi Vocational College (now Mississippi Valley State), where he finished his career. The Los Angeles Rams drafted him in the 14th round of the 1961 NFL Draft — he nearly slipped through entirely. Jones went on to invent the term "sack" and is widely regarded as the greatest pass rusher in NFL history, earning 8 Pro Bowls and induction into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1980. His college career was split between two HBCUs under dramatic circumstances, and without that chance to land at Mississippi Valley State, one of football's most revolutionary players might have disappeared entirely. The HBCU player of the year award (the Deacon Jones Trophy) is now named in his honor.
   Why this works for JOC: A "What Happened" story driven by systemic injustice (scholarship revoked for Civil Rights protest) with a triumphant ending; spans two HBCU programs; Deacon Jones is a legendary name that pulls NFL fans; perfect Black History Month content.
