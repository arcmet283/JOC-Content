Week of: October 20, 2026

TOP STORY THIS WEEK:
Jacobe Robinson — Grambling State
In the 2023 recruiting cycle, Jacobe Robinson was a 3-star quarterback from Henderson, Texas who chose Boston College (ACC) over scholarship offers from Jackson State and Alcorn State. At BC he appeared in just two games across two seasons — going 2-of-3 passing for 23 yards and scoring a rushing TD in a 56-0 blowout. He transferred to Southern Miss, barely played there either (2 games, 4 carries, no pass attempts in 2025). He then transferred to Grambling State, where head coach Mickey Joseph named him the starting QB heading into 2026. On October 18, 2026 (Week 7), Robinson threw four touchdown passes and scored a rushing TD to lead Grambling to a 45-38 upset over previously undefeated Jackson State (5-0) — the biggest single-game result in HBCU football this season. The arc is complete: the kid who passed on two HBCUs for the Power 5 dream spent three seasons unable to crack a lineup, then came back to the HBCU world and knocked the #1 HBCU team from the unbeaten ranks.
Hook angle: "He turned down Jackson State to play in the ACC. Two schools and three seasons later, he came back to knock Jackson State out of the unbeaten ranks."
Audience this serves: 18-34 (current-season hype, transfer portal generation), 25-44 (Power 5 vs. HBCU narrative, program loyalty themes)
Title format suggestion: Transfer — "He Turned Down Jackson State to Play in the ACC. Then Everything Changed."

3. "The First Black Quarterback Drafted in the First Round Never Got to Play Quarterback in the NFL" — Eldridge Dickey / Tennessee State [Classic Era, Pre-2000]
Story: In four seasons at Tennessee State (1965-1968), Eldridge Dickey led the Tigers to a 34-5-1 record, won the 1966 National Black College Championship on an undefeated season, and earned All-American honors twice. The Oakland Raiders made him the 25th overall pick in the 1968 NFL Draft — the first Black quarterback ever taken in the first round of an AFL or NFL draft. The Raiders immediately converted him to wide receiver. He never took a snap at QB in the pros, finishing his career with five catches for 112 yards and a touchdown. He died from a stroke on May 22, 2000, at age 54 — his position never validated, his potential never tested. His story is a direct antecedent to Doug Williams, Warren Moon, and every Black QB who fought for the right to play their position in the NFL.
Why this works for JOC: Pure "What Happened" format — dominant HBCU player, historic draft moment, quiet NFL erasure, early death; pairs naturally with the Doug Williams backlog story as a two-part "History of the Black QB" arc; pulls Tennessee State and Raiders search traffic from fans who've never heard this story.
