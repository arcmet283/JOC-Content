3. "He Turned Down Florida to Play QB at an HBCU — Then Became the Most Valuable Player in the NFL" — Steve McNair / Alcorn State — Classic Era (Pre-2000)
Story: Steve McNair was recruited out of Mount Olive, Mississippi, as a dual-threat quarterback who initially accepted a scholarship to the University of Florida to play running back. Instead, he chose Alcorn State — an HBCU in the SWAC — where he could play his natural position. What followed became one of the most extraordinary individual careers in college football history: McNair became the first and only player in NCAA history to gain more than 16,000 total yards in a career, averaged 400.5 yards of total offense per game, threw 119 career touchdowns, and appeared on the September 26, 1994, cover of Sports Illustrated under the headline "Hand Him the Heisman." He finished third in the Heisman vote (behind Rashaan Salaam and Ki-Jana Carter), won the Walter Payton Award, and was selected 3rd overall in the 1995 NFL Draft by the Houston Oilers. McNair went on to lead the Tennessee Titans to Super Bowl XXXIV, and in 2003 became the first African American quarterback to be named the NFL's Most Valuable Player. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame and the College Football Hall of Fame.
Why this works for JOC: One of the most powerful untouched HBCU stories available — the "turned down Florida for an HBCU" hook alone is cinematic; the Heisman near-miss, the NFL MVP first, and the Super Bowl run give this multi-chapter documentary structure; strong appeal to the 35-54 audience who remember "Air McNair."

4. "The HBCU Pass Rusher Who Broke the NFL's Sack Record" — Michael Strahan / Texas Southern — Comparison / Classic Era
Story: Michael Strahan grew up in Mannheim, Germany, where his father was stationed as an Army major, and returned to Texas for his senior year of high school with limited organized football experience. He earned a scholarship to Texas Southern University (SWAC), where he broke out as a pass rusher — setting a school-record 19 sacks in his senior season, earning Division I-AA Defensive Player of the Year honors, and back-to-back SWAC Player of the Year awards. The New York Giants selected him 40th overall in the 1993 NFL Draft. He went on to play 15 seasons with the Giants, set the NFL single-season sack record (22.5 in 2001), won Super Bowl XLII, and was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame. In January 2025, he became the first Texas Southern player ever inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame. The comparison title writes itself: "The HBCU Defensive End Who Set the NFL's Sack Record."
Why this works for JOC: Strahan's name drives massive search volume from NFL history and pop culture (GMA, FOX Sports), giving the channel exposure to people who've never heard of The JOC; the HBCU-to-Hall-of-Fame arc is classic JOC territory; and the 2025 College Football HOF induction makes it freshly relevant.
